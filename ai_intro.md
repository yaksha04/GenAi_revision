1. Definition of Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a branch of Computer Science that aims to create machines capable of performing tasks that normally require human intelligence.

These tasks include:

Learning
Reasoning
Decision-making
Problem-solving
Understanding language
Recognizing images and speech

2. History of AI

Understanding AI's history is important for exams.

1943: Birth of AI Concepts

Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts proposed the first mathematical model of an artificial neuron.

1950: Turing Test

Alan Turing proposed a test to determine whether a machine can exhibit intelligent behavior.

Turing Test

A human interacts with:

Another human
A machine

If the human cannot distinguish them, the machine passes the test.

1956: Birth of AI

The term Artificial Intelligence was officially coined at the:

Dartmouth Workshop

Led by:

John McCarthy
Marvin Minsky
1970–1980: Expert Systems

AI systems began solving specific problems using knowledge bases.

Example:

Medical diagnosis systems.

AI Winter

Funding and interest dropped because expectations exceeded actual results.

There were two major AI winters:

1974–1980
1987–1993
1997

Deep Blue defeated world chess champion Garry Kasparov.

2012

Deep Learning revolution began.

AI started achieving remarkable results in:

Image recognition
Speech recognition
NLP
2022-Present

Generative AI became mainstream.

Examples:

ChatGPT
Gemini
Claude

7. Ethical Considerations in AI

This is one of the most important modern AI topics.

A. Bias in AI

Bias occurs when AI treats groups unfairly.

Example:

Recruitment AI preferring one gender due to biased training data.

Causes
Biased datasets
Human prejudice
Poor data collection
Consequences
Discrimination
Unfair decisions
Loss of trust
B. Fairness

AI should provide equal treatment.

Example:

Loan approval systems should evaluate applicants fairly.

C. Transparency

Users should understand how AI makes decisions.

Example:

If a bank rejects a loan, the reason should be explainable.

D. Accountability

Someone must be responsible for AI decisions.

Questions:

Who is responsible if a self-driving car crashes?
The developer?
The company?

Intelligence

AI algorithms

Actuators
